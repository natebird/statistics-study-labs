Ground rules

Desmos in Bluebook

  • Type commands into the expression list, or open Add Item ▸ Inference (or type inference) to build a distribution or test from a menu.
  • Desmos labels each test T₁, T₂, …. Open the test's Significance Test section to set the null value and pick the Left, Both, or Right tail.
  • Read results by name: T₁.score (z, t, or χ²), T₁.dof, T₁.p (two-sided), T₁.pleft, T₁.pright, T₁.conf(0.95).lower and .upper.
  • .cdf(x) is always the area to the left of x. Use 1 - …cdf(x) for a right tail and .cdf(a, b) for an area between.

What earns credit

  • Calculator syntax alone, from either calculator, earns no credit. Name the distribution and its parameters, for example "X ~ N(40, 5), P(X > 48)", or name the procedure.
  • For inference, still state hypotheses, name the procedure, check conditions, report the test statistic, df, and p-value, and conclude in context.
  • Two-sample t procedures on both calculators use a decimal (Welch) df. The conservative df = smaller n − 1 is also accepted by hand. Pick one and be consistent.
  • Geometric distributions and chi-square goodness of fit are no longer in the course, so skip geodist, chisqgof, and χ²GOF-Test.

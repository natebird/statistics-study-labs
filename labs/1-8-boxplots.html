2. Key ideas

Five-number summary

Min, Q₁, median, Q₃, max

These five values split the data into four parts, each holding about 25% of the observations. The boxplot draws exactly these five numbers, plus any outliers.

Quarters

Longer piece ≠ more data

Each whisker and each half of the box holds about a quarter of the data. A longer section means those values are more spread out, not that there are more of them.

Outliers

Modified boxplots

On the AP exam, boxplots are usually modified: values beyond the fences are drawn as separate points, and each whisker stops at the most extreme value that is not an outlier.

Shape

Reading skew

If the right whisker and the right half of the box are longer, the distribution is likely skewed right. If the left side is longer, it is likely skewed left. Similar sides suggest rough symmetry.

Limits

What a boxplot hides

A boxplot does not show the sample size, gaps, clusters, or multiple peaks. Two very different data sets can have identical boxplots. Use a dotplot or histogram to see the full shape.

Percentiles

Cumulative graphs

The pth percentile is the value with p% of the data at or below it. On a cumulative relative frequency graph, go across from p% on the vertical axis, then down to the value.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Label the axis with the variable name and units, and use an even scale. A boxplot without a labeled scale does not get credit.
  • Before drawing, compute and show the fences: "Q₃ + 1.5(IQR) = 31 + 1.5(13) = 50.5, so 70 is an outlier."
  • Draw outliers as separate points, and end the whisker at the largest (or smallest) value that is not an outlier.
  • When you read a percentile, say what it means in context: "About 80% of commutes are 45 minutes or shorter."
  • When describing shape from a boxplot, say "appears" or "likely." A boxplot can hide clusters and gaps.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking the longer part of a boxplot contains more data. Every quarter holds about 25% of the values.
  • Drawing the whisker all the way to an outlier on a modified boxplot.
  • Reading the mean off a boxplot. The line in the box is the median.
  • Mixing up percent and percentile: "the 80th percentile" is a value of the variable, not a percent.
  • Claiming a boxplot shows the number of observations. It does not.

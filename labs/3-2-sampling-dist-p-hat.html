Sampling Distributions for Sample Proportions Study Lab

Every random sample gives a slightly different sample proportion p̂. The sampling distribution of p̂ describes all those values: it is centered at p, has standard deviation √(p(1 − p)/n), and is approximately normal when the Large Counts condition holds.

2. Key ideas

Center

μp̂ = p

On average, p̂ equals the true proportion. That makes p̂ an unbiased estimator of p.

Spread

σp̂ = √(p(1 − p)/n)

Spread shrinks as n grows: 4 times the sample size gives half the spread. It is largest when p = 0.5.

Shape

Large Counts

If np ≥ 10 and n(1 − p) ≥ 10, the sampling distribution is approximately normal. Otherwise it is skewed toward the side farther from 0 or 1.

10% condition

When sampling without replacement

Check n ≤ 0.1N. Then the observations are close enough to independent that the σ formula works.

Probability

Use the normal model

Standardize: z = (p̂ − p) ÷ σp̂, then find the area, or use mean p and SD σp̂ directly: Desmos 1 - normaldist(p, σ).cdf(cutoff) or TI-84 normalcdf(cutoff, 1E99, p, σ) for a right tail.

Three distributions

Population, sample, sampling distribution

The population has one p. A sample has its own p̂. The sampling distribution shows how p̂ varies over all samples of size n.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Describe a sampling distribution with shape, center, and spread, each justified: "approximately normal because 60(0.4) = 24 ≥ 10 and 60(0.6) = 36 ≥ 10."
  • Show the σ formula with numbers plugged in, not just the result.
  • For a probability, label the distribution: "Using N(0.4, 0.0632), P(p̂ > 0.5) = 0.057." Calculator syntax alone (Desmos 1 - normaldist(0.4, 0.0632).cdf(0.5) or TI-84 normalcdf(0.5, 1E99, 0.4, 0.0632)) does not earn credit.
  • Name both conditions and what each does: Large Counts for shape, 10% for the standard deviation formula.
  • Interpret in context: "There is about a 5.7% chance that more than half of a random sample of 60 students will say yes."

Common mistakes

  • Using p̂ instead of p in the Large Counts check when p is known. For sampling distributions, use the true p.
  • Mixing up the 10% condition (independence, σ formula) with Large Counts (normal shape).
  • Forgetting the square root in σ = √(p(1 − p)/n).
  • Saying a larger sample makes the sampling distribution "more normal" without the Large Counts numbers to back it up.
  • Confusing the distribution of one sample's data (yes/no answers) with the sampling distribution of p̂.

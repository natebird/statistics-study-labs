2. Key ideas

Center

x̄ is unbiased

The mean of the sampling distribution of x̄ equals the population mean: μx̄ = μ. Sample means do not systematically overshoot or undershoot μ, so x̄ is an unbiased estimator.

Spread

σx̄ = σ/√n

Averages vary less than single values. Larger samples give less variable sample means. To cut σx̄ in half you need four times the sample size, because of the square root.

10% condition

When the formula works

σ/√n assumes the observations are independent. When sampling without replacement, check that n is no more than 10% of the population size.

Shape

Normal population

If the population is normal, the sampling distribution of x̄ is exactly normal for any sample size, even n = 2.

Shape

Central limit theorem

If the population is not normal, the sampling distribution of x̄ becomes approximately normal as n grows. The usual guideline is n ≥ 30. Strongly skewed populations need larger samples.

Careful

Sample vs. sampling distribution

The distribution of one sample looks like the population, whatever n is. The sampling distribution is the distribution of x̄ across many samples. Only the second one gets narrower and more normal.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Describe the sampling distribution with all three: shape (with a reason), center (μx̄ = μ), and spread (σx̄ = σ/√n with the numbers plugged in).
  • Justify normality in context: "The population of battery lives is normal, so the sampling distribution of x̄ is normal," or "n = 40 ≥ 30, so by the CLT it is approximately normal."
  • Check the 10% condition when sampling without replacement: "40 is less than 10% of all batteries produced."
  • For a probability, show the model, the boundary, and the area: "N(50, 2.5); z = (54 − 50)/2.5 = 1.6; P(x̄ > 54) = 0.0548." Name the distribution and parameters if you write calculator syntax (Desmos 1 - normaldist(50, 2.5).cdf(54) or TI-84 normalcdf(54, 1E99, 50, 2.5)).

Common mistakes

  • Using σ instead of σ/√n when the question is about a mean of several values.
  • Saying the CLT makes the population or the sample normal. It is about the sampling distribution of x̄ only.
  • Applying the normal model to x̄ from a skewed population with a small n.
  • Thinking a larger sample changes μx̄. Only the spread and shape change.
  • Writing only calculator syntax like "1 − normaldist(50, 2.5).cdf(54)" (Desmos) or "normalcdf(54, 1E99, 50, 2.5)" (TI-84) with no labels. Identify each number; output from either calculator alone does not earn credit.

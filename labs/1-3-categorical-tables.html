2. Key ideas

Counts

Frequency table

Lists each category and how many individuals are in it. The counts add to the total number of individuals, n.

Proportions

Relative frequency

Count ÷ total. It can be written as a decimal (0.36) or percent (36%). The relative frequencies of all the categories add to 1, apart from rounding.

Compare

Why proportions?

Proportions put groups of different sizes on the same scale. 60 out of 100 and 60 out of 400 are very different results.

Center

Mode

The category with the highest frequency. It is the only measure of center that makes sense for categorical data.

Backward

From percent to count

Count = proportion × total, and total = count ÷ proportion. Many AP questions give you two of the three.

Context

Report in words

“36% of the 250 surveyed students chose fall” is a complete summary: statistic, group, and size.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Show the division: “48/120 = 0.40.” A bare number can lose the communication point.
  • Interpret in context with the denominator: “40% of the 120 students surveyed ride the bus.”
  • When comparing groups of different sizes, compare proportions, not counts, and say so.
  • Name the mode as a category (“Bus”), not as a number.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing the total by the count instead of the count by the total.
  • Comparing raw counts between groups of different sizes.
  • Reporting a mean or median for a categorical variable.
  • Forgetting that relative frequencies must add to 1, which is a quick way to find a missing value.

2. Key ideas

Definition

Probability, assuming H₀

The p-value is the probability of getting a statistic at least as extreme as the observed one, in the direction of Hₐ, assuming H₀ is true.

Evidence

Small p-value, strong evidence

A small p-value means the data would rarely happen if H₀ were true, which is evidence against H₀ and for Hₐ.

Direction

One tail or two

For Hₐ: p > p₀ use the upper tail; for p < p₀, the lower tail; for p ≠ p₀, double the tail beyond |z|. Upper tail: Desmos 1 - normaldist(0, 1).cdf(z), TI-84 normalcdf(z, 1E99, 0, 1).

Decision

Compare with α

If p-value < α, reject H₀ (statistically significant). If p-value ≥ α, fail to reject H₀. Never "accept" H₀.

What it is NOT

Not P(H₀ is true)

The p-value does not give the probability that a hypothesis is true or that results are due to chance. It assumes H₀ is true.

Simulation

p-values without formulas

Simulate many samples assuming H₀ and find the proportion that are at least as extreme as the observed result.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Interpret with three parts: the assumption ("assuming p = 0.10"), the probability, and "a sample proportion of 0.153 or greater by chance alone."
  • Link the decision to the comparison: "Because the p-value of 0.015 < α = 0.05, we reject H₀."
  • State the conclusion in context about the population: "There is convincing evidence that more than 10% of students at the school are left-handed."
  • For a simulation-based p-value, count the dots at least as extreme and divide by the number of simulated samples.
  • Show the area you found: "P(Z ≥ 2.16) = 0.0154." Desmos 1 - normaldist(0, 1).cdf(2.16) or TI-84 normalcdf(2.16, 1E99, 0, 1) finds it, but the calculator command alone does not earn credit: label the distribution, the boundary, and the direction.

Common mistakes

  • Saying the p-value is the probability that H₀ is true.
  • Forgetting to double for a two-sided alternative.
  • Writing "accept H₀" or "H₀ is true" when the p-value is large.
  • Using the wrong tail, e.g., P(Z ≤ z) for Hₐ: p > p₀.
  • Interpreting a p-value without the "assuming H₀ is true" condition.

2. Key ideas

Center

Centered at the true difference

The sampling distribution of p̂₁ − p̂₂ has mean μ = p₁ − p₂. That makes p̂₁ − p̂₂ an unbiased estimator of the difference in population proportions.

Spread

Variances add, not SDs

For independent samples, σ² = p₁(1 − p₁)/n₁ + p₂(1 − p₂)/n₂. You add the two variances, then take the square root. The variances add even though you subtract the proportions.

Shape

Large counts → normal

The shape is approximately normal when n₁p₁, n₁(1 − p₁), n₂p₂, and n₂(1 − p₂) are all at least 10.

Independence

Two conditions

The samples must be independent of each other (two separate random samples or random assignment), and each sample should be at most 10% of its population when sampling without replacement.

Sample size

Bigger n, less variability

σ shrinks as n₁ and n₂ grow. To cut σ in half you need about four times as many observations in each group.

Using it

Probability of a difference

With the normal model, find P(p̂₁ − p̂₂ > c) by computing z = (c − μ)/σ and using a normal table or a calculator. Desmos: 1 - normaldist(μ, σ).cdf(c) · TI-84: normalcdf(c, 1E99, μ, σ).

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Describe all three features with numbers and context: "The sampling distribution of p̂₁ − p̂₂ is approximately normal with mean 0.10 and standard deviation 0.069."
  • Check the large counts condition with all four products, and show the numbers.
  • State the 10% condition for each population when sampling without replacement.
  • For a probability, show the z-score: z = (0.15 − 0.10) ÷ 0.069 = 0.72, then P(Z > 0.72) = 0.236. On a calculator, Desmos: 1 - normaldist(0.1, 0.069).cdf(0.15) · TI-84: normalcdf(0.15, 1E99, 0.1, 0.069). Either way, label the inputs (mean, SD, boundary): calculator syntax alone does not earn credit.
  • Say why the samples are independent of each other: two separate random samples, or random assignment to two groups.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the standard deviations instead of the variances.
  • Subtracting the variances because the proportions are subtracted. Variability always adds.
  • Using sample proportions p̂ in the mean or SD formulas when the problem gives the true p.
  • Checking large counts only for successes. Failures count too.
  • Thinking a difference of 0 is guaranteed when p₁ = p₂. The center is 0, but samples vary.

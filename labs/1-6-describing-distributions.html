2. Key ideas

Shape

Symmetric or skewed

Symmetric: the two halves are near mirror images. Skewed right: a long tail toward larger values. Skewed left: a long tail toward smaller values. Name the skew for the tail, not the peak.

Peaks

Modes

Unimodal distributions have one peak, bimodal have two, and uniform distributions have no clear peak. Two peaks often mean two different groups are mixed together.

Center

Typical value

Report the median for skewed data or data with outliers, and the mean for roughly symmetric data. Give the value with units.

Variability

How spread out

Report the IQR with the median, or the standard deviation with the mean. The range is a quick but non-resistant measure.

Unusual

Outliers, gaps, clusters

Outliers are values that fall outside the overall pattern (check with the 1.5 × IQR rule). Gaps are empty stretches; clusters are groups of values separated by gaps.

Context

Name the variable

“The distribution of call length (minutes) is skewed right with a median of 4.6 minutes…” A description without context loses credit.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Use all four parts every time: shape, center, variability, unusual features, with the variable's name and units.
  • Give numbers, not just words: “median of 22 minutes, IQR of 12 minutes.” Estimates read from a graph are fine if they're reasonable.
  • When comparing distributions (topic 1.9), use comparison words like “greater than” or “more variable than,” not just two separate lists.
  • Back up an outlier claim with the 1.5 × IQR rule when you have the quartiles.

Common mistakes

  • Naming skew by where the peak is. A peak on the left with a tail to the right is skewed right.
  • Describing a bar chart (categorical data) as skewed or symmetric.
  • Saying “the range is 20 to 80.” The range is a single number: 80 − 20 = 60.
  • Leaving out context: “The data are skewed” earns less than “The distribution of phone call lengths is skewed right.”

2. Key ideas

Test statistic

How many standard errors away?

t = (x̄ − μ₀) ÷ (s/√n) measures how far the sample mean is from the null value, in standard errors. Values far from 0 are surprising if H₀ is true.

p-value

Probability, assuming H₀

The p-value is the probability, assuming H₀ is true, of getting a sample result at least as extreme as the one observed, in the direction of Hₐ. For ≠, count both tails.

Decision

Compare p with α

If p-value < α, reject H₀: the data give convincing evidence for Hₐ. If p-value ≥ α, fail to reject H₀: the data do not give convincing evidence for Hₐ.

Never "accept"

Failing to reject is not proof

A large p-value means the data are consistent with H₀, not that H₀ is true. Say "we do not have convincing evidence that…," never "we accept H₀."

Paired

Test the differences

For paired data, compute the differences in a stated order, then run a one-sample t test on them with μ₀ = 0 (usually) and df = number of pairs − 1.

Sample size

Big n finds small effects

With a huge sample, even a tiny difference from μ₀ can give a tiny p-value. Statistically significant does not always mean practically important.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Show the formula with numbers: t = (32.4 − 30) ÷ (5.1/√25) = 2.35, df = 24. Desmos or TI-84 output alone earns little credit: name the test and check conditions too.
  • Give the p-value and interpret it if asked: "Assuming the true mean delivery time is 30 minutes, there is about a 0.014 probability of getting a sample mean of 32.4 minutes or more by chance."
  • Link the decision to α explicitly: "Because 0.014 < 0.05, we reject H₀."
  • Conclude about Hₐ, in context, with uncertainty: "We have convincing evidence that the true mean delivery time is greater than 30 minutes."

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to double the one-tail area for a two-sided test.
  • Using the wrong tail, such as the area to the right when Hₐ is μ < μ₀.
  • Writing "we accept H₀" or "H₀ is true" when the p-value is large.
  • Saying "the p-value is the probability that H₀ is true." It is computed assuming H₀ is true.
  • For paired data, using two separate lists and df = n₁ + n₂ − 2 instead of the differences and df = pairs − 1.

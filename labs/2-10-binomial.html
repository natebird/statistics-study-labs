    2. Key ideas

    B

    Binary outcomes

    Each trial ends in one of two outcomes, called "success" and "failure." Success just means the outcome you are counting, even if it is something bad like a defect.

    I

    Independent trials

    Knowing the result of one trial does not change the probabilities for another. When sampling without replacement, use the 10% condition: n ≤ 10% of the population lets you treat trials as roughly independent.

    N

    Number of trials fixed

    You decide n in advance, like 10 free throws or 20 customers. If you keep going until a success, the count is not binomial.

    S

    Same probability

    The probability of success p is the same on every trial. A shooter who gets tired, or a deck that changes as cards are dealt, breaks this condition.

    Center and spread

    μ = np, σ = √(np(1 − p))

    The mean is the expected number of successes. The spread is largest when p = 0.5 and shrinks as p moves toward 0 or 1.

    Shape

    Skewed or symmetric?

    Small p gives a right-skewed distribution and large p a left-skewed one. When np ≥ 10 and n(1 − p) ≥ 10, the shape is approximately normal.

    4. On the FRQ

    How to earn the points

    • Define X and name the distribution with its parameters: "X = number of defective chips; X is binomial with n = 20 and p = 0.05."
    • Check the conditions in context, especially independence and the 10% condition when sampling without replacement.
    • Write the probability statement, then the work: "P(X ≥ 2) = 1 − P(X ≤ 1) = 1 − binomcdf(20, 0.05, 1) = 0.264" (Desmos: 1 − binomialdist(20, 0.05).cdf(1)). Calculator output alone doesn't earn credit: label the inputs (n, p, k) whether you use Desmos or TI-84 syntax.
    • Interpret μ = np as the long-run average count: "Over many batches of 20, the average number of defective chips is 1."
    • Answer the question asked: if they ask whether a result is surprising, compare its probability to a small cutoff and explain.

    Common mistakes

    • Using .cdf or binomcdf for "at least." Both always mean "at most," so P(X ≥ k) = 1 − binomialdist(n, p).cdf(k − 1) in Desmos, or 1 − binomcdf(n, p, k − 1) on the TI-84.
    • Off-by-one errors with "more than" and "fewer than."
    • Calling a count binomial when the number of trials is not fixed, such as "flip until the first head."
    • Forgetting the 10% condition when drawing people or items without replacement.
    • Writing only "binomcdf(20, .05, 1)" or "binomialdist(20, 0.05).cdf(1)" with no labels. Readers need to know what n, p, and k are.

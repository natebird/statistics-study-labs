2. Key ideas

Least squares

Smallest sum of squared residuals

Of all possible lines, the least-squares regression line (LSRL) makes Σ(y − ŷ)² as small as possible. Squaring keeps positive and negative residuals from canceling and penalizes big misses.

Formulas

Slope and intercept from summaries

b = r · sy ÷ sₓ and a = ȳ − b·x̄. These are on the AP formula sheet. The slope has the same sign as r.

Point of means

The line passes through (x̄, ȳ)

Substituting x̄ into the LSRL always gives ȳ. Moving one point changes x̄ and ȳ, so the whole line shifts.

r²

Coefficient of determination

r² is the fraction of the variation in y that is accounted for by the linear relationship with x. It is between 0 and 1. Interpret it as a percent, in context.

s

Typical prediction error

s, the standard deviation of the residuals, gives the typical distance between actual y-values and the line's predictions, in y's units.

Direction matters

Regressing y on x ≠ x on y

The LSRL minimizes vertical distances, so it depends on which variable is the response. Predicting x from y uses a different line, even though r is the same.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • When given summary statistics, show both formulas with numbers: "b = 0.87(6.2 ÷ 3.1) = 1.74; a = 70 − 1.74(5) = 61.3."
  • Write the final equation with ŷ or "predicted" and the variable names. A Desmos or TI-84 regression gives the numbers, but you still have to write and interpret the equation in context.
  • Read the slope and intercept from the Coef column of computer output; do not use the SE Coef or T values.
  • Interpret r² in context: "About 82% of the variation in exam scores is accounted for by the linear relationship with hours studied."
  • To find r from output, take √r² and give it the sign of the slope.

Common mistakes

  • Calling the LSRL the line that "goes through the most points." It minimizes the sum of squared residuals.
  • Using b = r · sₓ ÷ sy (flipped). The response's spread goes on top.
  • Interpreting r² as "the percent of points on the line" or "the percent of x explained by y."
  • Taking √r² and forgetting the sign: if the slope is negative, r is negative.
  • Assuming the line for predicting x from y is the same as the line for predicting y from x.

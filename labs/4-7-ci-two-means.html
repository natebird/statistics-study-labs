2. Key ideas

Parameter

Estimating μ₁ − μ₂

The interval estimates the difference between two population means. Always define it in context: "μ₁ − μ₂ = the true difference in mean exam score (app users − non-users)."

Standard error

Use s, not σ

We rarely know σ₁ and σ₂, so we plug in the sample standard deviations. The result, √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂), is the standard error. Using s instead of σ is why we use t* instead of z*.

Degrees of freedom

Calculator df vs. conservative df

Technology uses a formula that gives a df between the smaller n − 1 and n₁ + n₂ − 2. If you must use a table, use the smaller of n₁ − 1 and n₂ − 1. That gives a larger t* and a slightly wider, "conservative" interval.

Conditions

Random, 10%, Normal/Large

Random: two independent random samples or a randomized experiment. 10%: each sample is at most 10% of its population (when sampling). Normal/Large sample: each n ≥ 30, or the sample data show no strong skew or outliers.

Interpretation

The template

"We are C% confident that the interval from a to b captures the true difference in mean [variable] for [group 1] minus [group 2]." Say which group was subtracted from which.

Confidence level

A long-run capture rate

95% confidence means that if we repeated the sampling many times, about 95% of the intervals made this way would capture μ₁ − μ₂. It is not the probability that this one interval is right.

3. Quick reference

Critical values t* change with df. These are the values a table would give for a few df. For any df, Desmos tdist(df).inversecdf(0.975) or TI-84 invT(0.975, df) gives the 95% t*.

4. On the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • Name the procedure: "two-sample t interval for μ₁ − μ₂." Define μ₁ and μ₂ in context.
  • Check conditions with numbers: "n₁ = 32 ≥ 30 and n₂ = 30 ≥ 30," or "the dotplot of each sample shows no strong skew or outliers."
  • Do: write the formula with values plugged in, df, and the interval. "(82.4 − 78.9) ± 2.002√(7.1²/32 + 8.3²/30) = (−0.44, 7.44), df ≈ 57.2."
  • Conclude with the interpretation in context and the order of subtraction.
  • Calculator-only work (Desmos ttest with six summary values, or TI-84 2-SampTInt) can earn credit only if you name the procedure, check conditions, and give the inputs and the interval.

Common mistakes

  • Using z* instead of t* for means.
  • Pooling the standard deviations. On the TI-84, set Pooled: No; Desmos ttest(n₁, x̄₁, s₁, n₂, x̄₂, s₂) already uses the unpooled (Welch) df.
  • Using a two-sample interval for paired data (same subjects measured twice). That calls for a one-sample t interval on the differences.
  • Interpreting the interval as covering 95% of the individual differences or of the sample data.
  • Forgetting to say which way you subtracted. A negative interval means group 2's mean is likely larger.
